## Symbolic / intangible value, with IP / co-branding / fandom as imitation-resistant assets
- **In the articles**: [[Gielens 2025 -  copyconomy]] argues that symbolic-premium categories are the most vulnerable, and that resistance comes from community, service, ecosystems, trust.
- **My angle**: symbolic value is hard to copy; IP and fan-driven consumption can resist dupes？

## Authenticity / original vs imitation / dupes
- **My angle**: how fuzzy-boundary things get defined; how authenticity is judged.
- **In the articles**: [[Gielens 2025 -  copyconomy]] is dead center (superfakes, the original vs imitation line blurred or even irrelevant, the Gen Z authenticity paradox).
- **Relatedness: strong (direct)**. This is what the whole editorial is about. I can stand directly on it.

## 4. Co-branding / identity stacking / endorsement
- **My angle**: what happens when two brand or IP identities are layered together.
- **In the articles**: almost nothing. copyconomy only mentions influencer endorsement; the Scope A to Z has no co-branding entry.
- **Relatedness: gap (library barely covers it)**. This is the direction I keep returning to but the 10 pieces do not cover. It is the best candidate for a standalone entry or a research topic. See [[平台渠道权力]] only tangentially.

## 5. Newness and the two-sidedness of assortment refresh / velocity
- **My angle**: newness brings vitality and treasure-hunt feeling, but also habit disruption, exploration cost, choice overload, and dilution of the core image; it depends on category / shopper / format, and has a dynamic dimension (rate of change).
- **In the articles**: [[Gielens & Roggeveen 2023 - 什么是零售 JR范围]] lists newness as a legitimate topic; [[Gielens 2025 - 新购物悖论]] on velocity; [[Gielens 2026 - 我们不再看见的购物者]] on choice fragmentation; [[Gielens 2022 - 从一个颠覆到下一个 门店]] on curation vs endless aisle; trust and variance from Labor / Volatility / GenAI.
- **Relatedness: medium (parts present, nobody assembles them)**. Every part is findable somewhere, but braiding two-sidedness, structure, moderators, and the dynamic dimension into one umbrella is my integration, which the editorials do not do.

## 6. Attention / stimulation / addiction (including the "over-fishing" commons)
- **My angle**: high-stimulation marketing carries a hidden cost that returns to the merchant as lost trust; as everyone escalates, they deplete a shared pool of shopper attention and patience, an "over-fishing" problem.
- **In the articles**: [[Gielens 2025 - 新购物悖论]] (gamification / push turn every ideal into engagement then consumption, i.e. reflexive retail); [[Gielens 2026 - 我们不再看见的购物者]] (what the shopper really surrenders is time and attention).
- **Relatedness: strong, plus one escalation of mine**. The hidden-cost / long-run-harm point is direct. But treating attention as a **commons** depleted at the industry level is not made by the editorials, which stop at the individual level. That is my overlay, already captured in [[注意力经济与注意力公地]].

## 7. Physical space / environmental psychology (atmospherics to emotion / dwell to impulse)
- **My angle**: how physical and sensory environments shape emotion, dwell time, purchase, and even impulse / addiction.
- **In the articles**: [[Gielens 2022 - 从一个颠覆到下一个 门店]] (the store as experiential / social / curated space for meaningful interaction); the A to Z word list in [[Gielens & Roggeveen 2023 - 什么是零售 JR范围]] (atmospherics, displays, layout, packaging).
- **Relatedness: strong (direct), plus a contrast**. The articles support this directly. The bonus: Paradoxes locates addiction in the **digital** interface, while I locate it in **physical** sensory space, so the two form a natural comparison pair.

## 8. Stress to habit-driven behavior (exogenous shocks)
- **My angle**: stress (exam week, overtime, bad weather) shifts behavior from goal-directed to habitual.
- **In the articles**: only [[Breugelmans & Gielens 2025 - 零售波动 volatility]] touches panic / emotional buying under crisis.
- **Relatedness: weak (almost no match)**. And Volatility means macro crisis (pandemic, geopolitics), not the micro daily stress I mean. This is essentially my own consumer-psychology mechanism.

## Big picture

- **Strongly connected to the literature**: authenticity / dupes (3), attention / addiction (6), physical environment (7). You can plug straight into the editorials.
- **Mostly yours**: co-branding (4), pricing of intangible value (2), stress to habit (8), and half of attention-as-commons (6).
- **The largest open gap is co-branding (4)**, which is also the strongest candidate for a research topic.
