## Newness; assortment refresh / frequency
- **My**: newness brings vitality and treasure-hunt feeling, but also habit disruption, exploration cost, choice overload, and dilution of the core image; it depends on category / shopper / format, and has a dynamic dimension (rate of change).
- **In the articles**: [[Gielens & Roggeveen 2023 - What is retailing]] lists newness as a legitimate topic; [[Gielens 2025 - Efficiency and Responsibility]] on velocity; [[Gielens 2026 - Shopper]] on choice fragmentation; [[Gielens 2022 - Navigate Chaos]] on curation vs endless aisle; trust and variance from Labor / Volatility / GenAI.

## Attention / stimulation / addiction ("over-fishing" commons)
- **My**: high-stimulation marketing carries a hidden cost that returns to the merchant as lost trust; as everyone escalates, they deplete a shared pool of shopper attention and patience, an "over-fishing" problem. [[注意力经济与注意力公地]].
- **In the articles**: [[Gielens 2025 - Efficiency and Responsibility]] (gamification / push turn every ideal into engagement then consumption, i.e. reflexive retail); [[Gielens 2026 - Shopper]] (what the shopper really surrenders is time and attention).

## Brand Selection by GenAI
- **In the articles**: [[Geyskens Gielens Steenkamp - GenAI Brand Trust]] splits GenAI into platform GenAI (drawing on broad public information) vs retailer GenAI (drawing on retailer-controlled information), operating through two mechanisms, selection (which brands surface) and representation (how they are characterized and compared), which together form the query-contingent "AI shelf".
	- Informational authority
	- representational coherence
- **My**:
	- Temporal and coverage bias: the framework implicitly assumes the training / retrieval corpus covers time windows and languages / regions evenly, which it may not. What happens to a brand that goes viral right after the training cutoff? (parametric memory vs live retrieval / RAG, recency handling)
	- Are the drivers independent? Does a brand with high informational authority necessarily have high representational coherence? A brand can be very present yet described inconsistently.
	- Private label advantage: since retailer GenAI leans on retailer-controlled information, do private labels get a structural edge there, because the retailer effectively authors the corpus?
	- Trust in the output: how much do people actually believe an AI-shelf recommendation, and does that trust differ between platform GenAI and retailer GenAI?
	- Retailer GenAI vs classic recommender: how does brand selection inside retailer GenAI differ from a traditional recommendation algorithm, and is it tied to product availability (in stock) the way ranking algorithms are?
