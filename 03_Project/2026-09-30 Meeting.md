# Meeting 2026-09-30

## Last Meeting [[2026-09-23 Meeting]]

Summary: four topics, each mapped by stakeholder trade-offs.

### Topic 1: Treasure-hunt merchandising
- Mechanism: retailers manufacture constant newness (shelf rotation, flyers and cyclical promos, Temu-style gamified shopping, Action, Lidl Middle Aisle) to lift purchase frequency.
- Core question: when does this stimulate-to-consume loop shift from boosting welfare to "too much"? Trade-offs?
- Retailer: upside is margin, foot traffic, low inventory pressure; risk is higher because assortment quality may not be stable.
- Brand: clears excess stock, exposure in lower-tier markets; but hurts brand image.
- Consumers: value for money, treasure hunting; but impulse buying and weak after-sales guarantee.

### Topic 2: Dupe
- Biggest winners are retailers: fast-follow fast-fashion giants, white-label e-commerce, private-label grocers (Aldi, Trader Joe's); they cut heavy R&D and brand-marketing cost and seize share at extreme low prices. Trade-off: legal exposure and brand trust.
- Brand: every dupe depends on the original existing; the flood of "dupe" talk actually raises the original's fame, and forces big brands off pure price premium toward an absolute gap on core tech, craft, key ingredients, or after-sales. Downside: profit and market share eroded.
- Social media: upside is a traffic magnet (high reach and engagement); platforms embed shop links (e.g. TikTok Shop) for instant monetization and large commissions. Downside: false marketing and spam flood in, damaging ecosystem and content authenticity.
- Consumers: upside is similar experience at low price, plus feeling like a smart buyer. Downside: cheap unit price triggers a "buying is winning" mindset and unrestrained stockpiling (total on many imperfect dupes can equal one excellent genuine item); plus uncertain quality and after-sales.

### Topic 3: Co-branding and licensing
- Retailer: breaks into new circles, brings new traffic, creates urgency, shapes brand image. Downside: licensing fees and sales pressure; co-branded goods are highly time-sensitive, once hype passes (e.g. a film leaves theaters) unsold stock becomes dead inventory.
- IP side (brands / collaborators / licensors): upside is monetization, lower-tier market reach, buzz. Downside: brand dilution if over-licensed (visual fatigue when the IP is everywhere); reputational contagion, a licensed retailer's problems directly tarnish the brand/IP.
- Consumers: upside is emotional value and identity. Downside: scarcity framing drives impulse buying or forced scalper markups, plus paying the co-brand premium.

### Topic 4: Virtual goods / digital products
- Game skins, streaming memberships, cloud storage, application software, e-books, AI tool subscriptions

### Topic 1: Low-effort shopping

- "Low-effort shopping" with other trends
- Hard discounters, warehouse clubs, private label keep winning, not just on fair prices.
- Deeper reason (my view): what shoppers really dislike is search + vigilance/effort cost, not price itself.
- The fatigue = filtering many substitutable, homogeneous options + fear of missing out.


- The feeling a retailer/brand should give: trust that "the default is fair."
- So no need to: compare prices, compute unit prices, wait for a sale, or suspect the "original price" is inflated.

**poles of effort**
- Low-effort pole: non-substitutable, coherent, trusted; the retailer is the brand (IKEA, grocery private label); tightly curated assortment; every item trusted.
- High-effort pole: infinite, substitutable, exhausting; the open homogeneous shelf (multi-brand sales, browsing clothes online, Temu, dupes, frequent newness).
- Links to "when does something become too much?", incl. treasure-hunting.

**effort dimensions**
- Price guarantee, will it be cheaper next week?
- Is the discount real?
- Shrinkflation
- Returns / exchanges
- Uneven quality

**Next steps**
- Which retailer practices lower shoppers' "effort" cost, and how does that translate into long-term well-being?
- Worth pursuing as a direction?

### Topic 2: AI

Sources: EuroCommerce; McKinsey x EuroCommerce; Coresight Research (Groceryshop 2026).

**0. Landscape by region**

Protocol: UCP (Universal Commerce Protocol), open AI-commerce standard from Google + Shopify, early 2026. Merchants expose one standard interface, AI reads real-time inventory, applies loyalty discounts, completes checkout on the shopper's behalf; can merge orders across platforms; whole flow stays in-chat, with a secure in-chat confirmation for one-tap pay + delivery booking. (https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp)

US
- Nov 2025, Target x OpenAI: Target on the OpenAI platform, invoked inside ChatGPT; also rolled out "Store Companion" AI assistant to hundreds of thousands of associates across ~2,000 stores. (https://openai.com/zh-Hant/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/)
- Jan 2026, Walmart x Google: deep AI partnership (Furner + Pichai); Walmart catalog integrated into Gemini. US only.
- Jan 2026, Google Cloud / Gemini Enterprise unifies shopping + customer service: Kroger, Lowe's, Woolworths.
- Mar 2026, Best Buy x OpenAI: catalog deeply integrated into ChatGPT.
- Jul 2026, OpenAI "Advertise in ChatGPT" self-serve portal (Jul 22); early advertisers Best Buy, Lowe's, VistaPrint.

Europe
- Jan 2026, Frasers Group x Microsoft: advancing AI-driven retail search.

Southeast Asia
- Jun 2026, Shopee in ChatGPT app, 8 core markets (SG, MY, PH, ID, TH, VN, TW + Brazil). Natural-language picks, one-tap to Shopee checkout. Enterprise AI tools for eligible SME sellers: auto-generate listings + marketing copy, automate customer service.

China
- Aug 2025, AI virtual-human 24/7 livestream boom.
- Apr 2026, JD fresh-grocery AI assistant.
- Aug 2026, Taobao AI shopping assistant (monitors inventory, places orders).

**1. Consumer adoption, AI is already in the journey**
- EU consumers using AI for discovery/evaluation: 61% (some) / "about half" (others).
- Expected young-skewed; actual appeal spans all ages.
- Last 2 to 3 yrs: search share shifting sharply, traditional to agentic.
- Funnel: 84% use AI daily, 38% for product research/purchase decisions, ~15% used AI tools on retailer sites, 1 to 3% have ordered via an AI platform.

**2. Role of the assistant, intent + choice overload**
- Understands shopping intent; shift from searching SKUs to solving problems; grocery especially suited.
- More choices/products/fulfillment options (same-day pickup) means harder to decide; assistant positioned to resolve the choice friction retailers created themselves.
- Next phase, suggestion to action: personalized meal plans, pre-loaded baskets, conversational preference updates, coupon activation, store switching.

**3. Effectiveness metrics**
- DoorDash (Ask DoorDash): launch cart +35%, checkout 3x faster, later +50%, 5x faster. 
- Kroger: simplifying is not the same as smaller cart.
- Zalando: real-time behavioral data + graph recommenders + gen-AI assistants (homepage ranking, outfits, sizing), ~20% of recent revenue growth, returns down up to 7%.
- AI-using customers ordered 60% more unique SKUs.
- Mechanism: conversational is not feed/search. Open-ended questions ("what can I make with these fridge ingredients?") may surface a broader relevant set, vs front-loading paid placements / highest-signal brands.
- Double-edged: same rec-sys can concentrate attention on a few algorithm-favored products, shelf-position fight becomes a harder-to-audit algorithmic-ranking fight.

**4. Agentic commerce, 3 waves**
- Wave 1, GEO (Generative Engine Optimization): like SEO; optimize how products, content, brand signals are read + surfaced by AI models.
- Wave 2, Orchestrated commerce: AI on AI platforms and retailer/brand sites, brand-native experiences across search, choice, purchase, delivery. E.g., John Lewis (£800M transformation) pushing catalog onto AI platforms; Tesco in-app assistant generates meal plans + dynamically builds baskets (~280k employees tested first).
- Wave 3, Autonomous commerce: agents authorized to order independently, concierge-like (monitor household stock, auto-reorder); US Walmart/Etsy lead, Europe still on waves 1 to 2.
