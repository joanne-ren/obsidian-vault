### Topic 2: AI

#### 1. How people use AI for shopping

**Role in the journey**
- AI chat and traditional search are used interchangeably within the same stage; 42% of requests are exploratory, attraction/destination-type needs; AI complements rather than replaces search. ("Shopping with a Platform AI Assistant: Who Adopts, When in the Journey, and What For")

**Impact on online behavior**
- "The Impact of LLM Adoption on Online User Behavior" — Padilla, Lam, Lambrecht & Hollenbeck (2025/26). Comscore clickstream, 1M+ users, detailed 2022–2023 data.
	- ~20 weeks after adopting an LLM, traditional search is 20%+ below pre-adoption.
	- High-traffic sites are unaffected, but smaller sites show signs of significant traffic drops.
	- Display-ad impressions fall, especially among heavy online shoppers.

**AI recommendation to purchase path**
- "From Prompt to Purchase: How AI Brand Recommendations Move Consumers on the Open Web" — Iannelli & Ai (2026). User-authorized clickstream stitched to the same user's ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini conversations (users see brands they never typed; some then go search).
	- After AI recommends a brand to users not previously exposed to it: within 7 days brand search +4.3pp, brand-site visits +2.4, retailer-page visits +1.0; most of this path is missed by last-click attribution.
	- Method: opt-in volunteers get a unified identity tag; the same user's timeline is matched at the individual level (e.g., recommended "Garmin" in ChatGPT at 2:00pm, then a Google search for "Garmin" or an Amazon visit at 2:05pm).

**Adoption size**
- "Europe's Agentic Commerce Moment" — McKinsey (2 Mar 2026). French, German, UK consumers.
	- 63% use AI to compare brands/models/prices/reviews; 55% to learn a category; 46% for inspiration; 56% accept AI suggestions but decide themselves; 47% unwilling to let AI auto-manage repurchases.
- "Shopping in the Age of AI: Redefining Stores for a New Era" — McKinsey & ICSC (Apr 2026). 3,004 US consumers, 7–11 Jan 2026, plus executive interviews.
	- 68% used at least one AI tool in the past three months; US agentic commerce up to ~$1 trillion by 2030.
- "Consumers Trust AI to Buy Better—Brands Must Adapt" — BCG (2025/26). Global Consumer Radar longitudinal survey + AI-assisted qualitative interviews.
	- Shopping is the 3rd-largest GenAI use; shopping-related use grew 35% (Feb–Nov 2025); 60%+ highly trust GenAI output; India 64% use GenAI during purchase.

**Trust**
- Consumer trust in AI is not blind; it builds through a dynamic "trust calibration" process.

**Query patterns (not shopping-specific)**
- AI Mode queries average ~3x longer than traditional search; follow-ups growing 40%+ per month. Common opening words: what, how, I, is, can ("I" openers suggest users treat it as conversation). "which" openers resolve ~40% faster than average (mostly "which of"/"which one"); "where should I" and "ideas for" queries are rising.
- [Amazon Rufus](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/how-to-use-amazon-rufus) — four typical question types: single item (is this coffee machine easy to clean? is this mascara clean beauty?), comparison (gas vs. wood-fired pizza oven?), own orders (when did I last buy sunscreen?), scenario-driven (what do I need for a summer party?).
- [OpenAI shopping research](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-shopping-research/) — two query types: simple (check price, confirm a feature) and deep (comparisons, multiple constraints, trade-offs); deep queries concentrate in parameter-rich categories (electronics, beauty, home & garden, kitchen appliances, sports & outdoors).

**Region / geography**
- Model-level studies (API / open weights) almost all inject geography via the prompt (country persona, "assume you are from country X", asking in the target language), because API calls don't carry the end user's location.
- Audits of consumer-facing chat products (ChatGPT web, Copilot, Gemini) use residential proxies / VPN / fixed institutional IPs plus browser language settings.
- Declaring a country in the prompt does pull output toward local, but brings stereotyping.
- The same model differs greatly between web UI and API (recommendation sources can be almost non-overlapping), so API-only findings don't extrapolate to what real users see.
- AI Forensics / AlgorithmWatch (2023, grey lit): audited Bing Chat on the Swiss federal and German state elections via VPN + Swiss/German residential IPs, grouped by language (DE/FR/EN); an early case using IP location as a design variable in GenAI audits.
- Kamruzzaman et al. (2024): a "country-of-origin effect" — stating the user's country in the prompt makes the model prefer that country's local brands; the bias direction can reverse.
- OpenAI's web-search tool and Perplexity's Sonar API both support a user_location parameter (country and city).
