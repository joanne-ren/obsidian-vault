### Topic: ESL and trust

**Trust as premise and asset**
- Shoppers who believe the default is fair don't watch prices while shopping.
- Two sides: for the shopper, trust is saved effort; for the retailer, trust is an asset, shoppers who aren't price-aware pay the regular price.

- Store loyalty (share of wallet, trip frequency, churn) & price awareness 
	- A shopper can keep coming because the store is close, while at the same time watching prices more. 

**Channels through which ESL may change behavior**
- Signal: shopper thinks "they can change prices anytime now" → more price-aware, even if actual prices didn't move.
- Actual pricing: re-pricing is now costless → store really re-prices more often / runs more promos → shopper is just responding to real change.
- Easier comparison: labels carry unit price, promo, QR code → comparison because its cost dropped, not because of distrust.

Store positioning
- Signal: stronger at EDLP stores, their whole proposition is "stable, trustworthy prices".
- Actual-pricing: stronger at Hi-Lo stores, easier for them to add promotions.
- Comparison channel: mostly a category thing, stronger where unit price is hard to compare; little to do with store positioning.

**Awareness measure**
- Elasticity / stockpiling are near-useless for must-buy staples (milk, bread): quantity doesn't move.
- More general: gap of each purchase price from the lowest price available at the time (gap-to-lowest-available-price).
- More price-aware shoppers buy more often at the low point → the gap shrinks.
- Doesn't rely on being able to stockpile or on promotions existing; covers all categories.

**Identification concern**
- Rollout order may be non-random (big stores / high-competition stores first); 
	- find an exogenous reason for deployment (supplier capacity)？
