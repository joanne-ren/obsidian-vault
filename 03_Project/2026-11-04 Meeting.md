# Meeting 2026-11-04

Shoppers using an AI assistant to search for products and to receive recommendations.

Four questions have to be answered before a dataset is built, and this report takes them in turn: we first ask whether shoppers are in fact using AI this way; we then examine what their prompts look like and where such prompts can be found; we check what computer-science research has established about shopping AI; and we conclude with the design conditions, independence and regional validity, under which a self-built dataset could be trusted.

---

## 1. Are shoppers using AI to shop, and is this a trend?

At first glance the answer is an unambiguous yes. Vendors report that shoppers who use their assistants behave measurably differently: DoorDash states that AI users order 60% more unique SKUs, which points to broadened discovery rather than faster repurchasing ([Grocery Dive](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/takeaways-groceryshop-conference-2026-las-vegas/831421/); [Coresight](https://coresight.com/research/groceryshop-2026-day-two-ai-moves-into-action-as-retailers-redefine-value-and-commerce/)); Kroger found that assistant appeal spans all age groups and that simplifying one friction point tends to send shoppers exploring further ([Grocery Dive](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/kroger-ai-technology-groceryshop/831102/)). Independent surveys converge on the same order of magnitude. McKinsey's agentic-commerce survey (≈21,000 consumers across 11 markets) reports 53% already shopping with AI,; in the most recent purchase, AI chat or search tools were cited as the single largest influence by 29% of AI users, three times the share citing a traditional search engine ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/who-wins-when-ai-does-the-shopping-new-evidence-on-agentic-commerce)). Deloitte (39% use generative AI for at least one holiday task), Adobe (42% of US shoppers have used an AI assistant to shop), Salesforce (39% of shoppers, 54% of Gen Z, for product discovery), ICSC × McKinsey (68% used at least one AI tool in the past three months), and BCG (shopping now the third-largest use of generative AI, up 35% in 2025) all read the same way ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/retail-distribution/holiday-retail-sales-consumer-survey.html); [Adobe via TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/16/ai-traffic-to-us-retailers-rose-393-in-q1-and-its-boosting-their-revenue-too/); [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/consumer-shopping-ai-trends-2025/); [ICSC × McKinsey](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/icsc-mckinsey-company-release-shopping-in-the-age-of-ai-redefining-stores-for-a-new-era-report); [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/consumers-trust-ai-to-buy-better-brands-must-adapt)).

Yet the headline adoption rate conceals a funnel that narrows sharply toward the bottom. McKinsey × EuroCommerce finds that while roughly 84% use AI daily and 38% use it for product research, only about 15% have used AI tools on a retailer's own site and a mere 1–3% have actually placed an order through an AI platform ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/europes-agentic-commerce-moment-decision-influence-is-here-execution-is-coming)); Deloitte records the same gap from the other side, with only 16% willing to let AI complete a purchase. The relevant question, therefore, is not whether adoption is high but at which stage of the journey the assistant is used. On the present evidence, shoppers use AI mainly to discover and evaluate; autonomous ordering remains early. A platform-assistant study of 31 million Ctrip users sharpens the point: attraction-type, exploratory requests account for 42% of all queries, and the assistant is used to complement conventional search rather than to replace it (Shopping with a Platform AI Assistant, [arXiv:2603.24947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24947)).

Supply runs ahead of use. Platforms and retailers are wiring catalogs into AI entry points faster than shoppers are transacting through them, and a connective standard is forming around Google and Shopify's Universal Commerce Protocol ([UCP](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp)). Deployment now spans regions unevenly: the United States leads (Target inside ChatGPT, [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/); Walmart and Sam's Club inside Gemini, [Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/01/11/walmart-and-google-turn-ai-discovery-into-effortless-shopping-experiences); Best Buy inside ChatGPT, [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/09/16/best-buy-opens-up-gift-buying-and-discover-through-chatgpt-ahead-of-holidays/)); Europe lags (Frasers via the Algolia × Microsoft stack, [Algolia](https://www.algolia.com/about/news/algolia-collaborates-with-microsoft-to-drive-real-time-product-data-to-shopping-experiences)); Southeast Asia arrives through Shopee in ChatGPT across eight markets ([TechNode](https://technode.global/2026/06/22/sea-limited-openai-to-jointly-expand-ai-access-for-e-commerce-shopee-users-sellers/)); and China pushes furthest into autonomy, with Alibaba's Qwen able to compare and place orders across Taobao and Tmall ([Alibaba](https://www.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-1991231293551017984)). In sum, shoppers are using AI, and the trend is real, but it concentrates at the top of the funnel, and wide deployment is not the same as wide use.

## 2. What do shoppers' prompts look like, and where can they be found?

The available material documents at length how wisely to instruct an agent yet says little about how real shoppers instruct one. The first category is researcher-designed and reusable off the shelf, a screenshot-based shopping-agent system prompt that judges only on title, price, ratings, and review count and is told to ignore product position ([arXiv:2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630)); the three forced-format protocols of the GEO bias experiments, from top-K ranking to pairwise choice ([arXiv:2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17443)); and persona templates that manipulate geography by swapping a single location line. These are experiment scaffolds, not records of natural behavior.

What natural behavior exists appears only as scattered profile, not as a corpus. Industry observation describes shoppers stating broad needs ("a healthy meal my kids will like, under \$40"; dinner from fridge ingredients) and shifting from SKU search toward problem-solving. Query-pattern studies add texture: AI-mode queries run roughly three times longer than traditional search with follow-ups rising month over month; Amazon's Rufus sees four recurring question types, single-item, comparison, own-order, and scenario-driven ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/how-to-use-amazon-rufus)); and OpenAI distinguishes simple from deep queries, the latter clustering in parameter-rich categories such as electronics, beauty, and kitchen ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-shopping-research/)). Recommendations move across turns: ask for the best first-marathon running shoe and a few familiar brands surface; add a budget, flat feet, and winter pavement, and by the fourth turn the first-named brand has often vanished. Each added constraint turns a category question into a demand space, who is buying, for what occasion, under what constraints, toward what outcome, which implies that a dataset must preserve the full multi-turn exchange and log the constraints that define each demand space ([Joachimsthaler, 2026](https://medium.com/@ejoachimsthaler/ai-will-not-kill-brands-it-will-expose-weak-ones-9b64de04e0c1)).

Our target data can be specified precisely as the product of three axes: prompt (asked by a real shopper, or simulated from real questions) × reply (what the model returned at the time, or re-run afterward) × content (shopping-related, with a product recommendation in the answer). This yields three types, (i) real question with its original answer, (ii) real question re-run, and (iii) designed question re-run. A first tranche of sources already exists, ordered by how directly they can be used: ConsumerQ is the closest fit, extracting real purchase-advice requests and shipping a coding manual for prompt taxonomy ([arXiv:2609.18729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18729)); donated full histories are the cleanest but demand recruitment and filtering (InVivoGPT, [arXiv:2602.01114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01114); the Dutch D3I/Port donation infrastructure, [uu.nl](https://www.uu.nl/en/organisation/methodology-and-statistics/data-donation); the region-specific Global South corpus, [arXiv:2609.38279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38279)); large public corpora are free but require filtering a shopping subset (WildChat-4.8M, [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/how2everything/WildChat-4.8M); Search Arena 24k, [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/search-arena-24k); ShareChat, [arXiv:2512.17843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17843)); and SparkToro × Gumshoe captures volunteers phrasing their own prompts and records what they saw ([blog](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/)). The gap that remains is specific: most of these are not shopping-purpose-built, few carry a prompt taxonomy (budget, dietary, occasion constraints; whether identity, region, or preference is disclosed), and corpora that are real at both ends, real prompt and the reply the shopper actually saw, stay scarce. 

Finally, paid instruments may support collection on their own way: Grips Intelligence reports channel-level sales down to one row per product × retailer × day, including ChatGPT-referred volume ([method](https://gripsintelligence.com/data-methodology)); Similarweb tracks AI-chatbot traffic to any site ([link](https://aisearch.similarweb.com/ai-chatbot-traffic/)); and Ahrefs Brand Radar exposes brands and citations in AI answers, though its prompts are rewritten search queries rather than what shoppers typed ([docs](https://docs.ahrefs.com/en/api/reference/brand-radar/get-ai-responses)). Because such tools disagree, two-source cross-validation is the safer practice.

## 3. What is computer-science research finding about shopping AI?

Research clusters around two mechanisms, recommendation bias and agent behavior. 

On bias, Chu and Hou's Incumbent Advantage tests three commercial models on skincare, a category judged on reputation rather than pre-purchase inspection ([arXiv:2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17443)). When specifications are identical, the well-known brand is recommended every time; grant a rival a rating edge of less than a tenth of a star and that monopoly evaporates. Authority-style marketing language, including fabricated clinical claims, can also unseat the incumbent, and when every brand adopts the same generative-engine-optimization tactics, each brand's payoff collapses from 0.802 to 0.007 while non-participants receive no recommendations at all, a prisoner's dilemma in which everyone must play and, once all play, no one wins.

On behavior, Allouah and colleagues audit shopping agents in controlled marketplaces with the ACES framework ([arXiv:2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630)) and find that agent choice is a different kind of decision-maker rather than a rational one: agents carry strong position bias that persists even in text-only interfaces, so no "top rank" is universal; they consistently penalize sponsored tags while rewarding platform endorsements; they concentrate demand on a few "modal" products; their preferences reshuffle when the model updates; and a seller-side agent making query-conditional description tweaks can lift its share. A second line of empirical work rounds out the picture, recommendations treated as a stochastic retrieval-and-ranking process that proves highly unstable (Malthouse et al., [arXiv:2609.16304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16304)); a consistent global-over-local brand bias whose direction can reverse once the user's country is stated (Kamruzzaman et al., [ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.707/)); and synthetic dialogue datasets built to train and evaluate agents rather than to measure bias (Wizard of Shopping, [arXiv:2502.00969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00969); ShopDial, [arXiv:2504.14375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14375)).

Two further results bear on visibility. First, AI recommendations demonstrably move shoppers on the open web: when an assistant recommends a previously unseen brand, that brand's searches rise +4.3pp within seven days along a path that is largely invisible to last-click attribution (Iannelli & Ai, [arXiv:2606.10907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10907)), while LLM adoption cuts a user's traditional search by more than 20% and drains traffic from smaller sites (Padilla et al., [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5393256)); an AI summary alone drops the click-through on a traditional result from 15% to 8% ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)). Second, and most consequential for a dataset, being seen is not the same as being chosen: in AIVO's multi-turn probes a brand appears in 95.7% of early answers but survives as the final recommendation only 12.7% of the time, and across repeated identical prompts the cited sources change 95.8% of the time while the final recommendation changes only 14.6% ([AIVO, industry evidence, not peer-reviewed](https://www.aivojournal.org/appeared-in-96-of-journeys-chosen-in-13/)). The paradox is that the same recommendation engine that broadens discovery can also concentrate attention on a few favored products. The measurement lesson follows directly: record the final recommendation rather than the mention, and treat any single output as one draw from a distribution that must be sampled repeatedly.

## 4. Building dataset: independence and regional validity

### Independence (no history)

Whether one query is contaminated by a prior one, or by someone else's, depends on which of four layers the state lives in, and genuine carry-over sits almost entirely in the first two: context (explicit conversation history in the same thread), account/product (memory, personalization, geographic and language signals), service (caching, batching, silent model updates), and statistical (whether repeated samples of one prompt may be treated as i.i.d.). The lower two layers are time-correlated noise and drift; they do not carry content forward.

Collection should therefore follow a fixed protocol:

1. One stateless API call is the unit of observation, one trial, one fresh request, the full prompt; never multiple trials in one thread, since multi-turn context shifts behavior by about 39% (Laban et al., [arXiv:2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)). In practice, use the Responses API with store=false.
2. temperature=0 with a fixed seed is not determinism, repeat-sample each prompt and report stability metrics.
3. Log the model snapshot, date, system_fingerprint, and every parameter, because a same-named service drifts over short periods (Chen, Zaharia & Zou, [arXiv:2307.09009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009)).
4. Treat the consumer UI and the API as two different experimental subjects, their system prompts, moderation, memory, search, and geography differ, and to study what shoppers actually see, use sock-puppet accounts or logged-out sessions with controlled IP and language.
5. Cluster by prompt/item: N repeats of one prompt are not N independent observations, so use cluster-robust standard errors and paired differencing (Miller, [arXiv:2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640); naive SEs can understate uncertainty threefold).
6. Repeat N times per prompt × condition and report raw and parsed agreement rates with variance and intervals (Atil et al., [arXiv:2408.04667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667)); a generalizability D-study puts reliability at about 0.58 at five repeats, 0.74 at ten, and 0.81 at fifteen (Żatuchin, [arXiv:2609.04047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04047)).
7. Randomize trial order and interleave conditions within one time window, blocking by time and adding time fixed effects for long collection.
8. Vary wording: run several semantically equivalent phrasings and report a range rather than one prompt's result (Sclar et al., [arXiv:2310.11324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324)).

### Regional validity (geography as a controlled variable)

Geography enters differently by access method. At the model level (API or open weights), studies inject location through the prompt, a country persona, an "assume you are from X," a target-language question, because the API call carries no end-user location (as in Kamruzzaman's country personas or the GEO experiment's California/Shanghai swap). Audits of consumer products instead manipulate residential proxies, VPNs, or fixed institutional IPs together with browser language, a design pioneered when AI Forensics and AlgorithmWatch audited Bing Chat across Swiss and German IPs grouped by language ([report](https://aiforensics.org/work/bing-chat-elections); logged-out ChatGPT vs API, [arXiv:2608.30052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30052)). Declaring a country pulls the output local but introduces stereotyping, Kamruzzaman's country-of-origin effect ([ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.707/)). Because UI and API diverge so far that their recommendation sources may barely overlap, API-only findings do not extrapolate to real shoppers (Kirgis et al., [arXiv:2604.06188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06188)). Where a built-in control exists it should be used and logged: both OpenAI web-search ([docs](https://developers.openai.com/docs/guides/tools-web-search)) and Perplexity's Sonar API ([docs](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/user-location-filter-guide)) accept a user_location parameter for country, city, and region.

### Hidden-state hazards to log or neutralize

The independence protocol maps onto a catalog of concrete hazards, each with a mitigation: same-thread history (one request per trial; Laban et al. 2025, Tjuatja et al. [arXiv:2311.04076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04076)); server-side session state in the Responses API (store=false; [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/conversation-state)); ChatGPT memory and chat-history references that survive a deleted chat ([OpenAI Memory](https://openai.com/index/memory-and-new-controls-for-chatgpt/)); account-metadata profiling ([Willison, 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/May/21/chatgpt-new-memory/)); memory-induced sycophancy (Jain et al., [arXiv:2509.12517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12517)); exit IP and query language that decide which market's products surface ([arXiv:2608.30052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30052)); and interface-layer differences between UI and API (Kirgis et al. [arXiv:2604.06188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06188); Encarnación et al. [arXiv:2608.06202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06202); Lipphardt et al., [Dual Standards](https://petsymposium.org/foci/2026/foci-2026-0004.php)).

## Collection method

### Scope
- Object: the behavior of shoppers using an AI assistant to search for retail products and receive recommendations; we record the prompt and the reply.
- Goal: simulate and collect daily, running for 6 to 12 months to observe the trend, across regions.

### Design
- Interface: API only
- Prompt design:
  - Category (electronics, beauty, grocery, apparel, home).
  - Demand-space constraints (budget, dietary, occasion, use).
- Models: API-accessible only.
- Regions: to be discussed. An API call carries no end-user location, so region can only be injected, through:
  - a country persona written into the prompt;
  - the query language;
  - the user_location parameter (OpenAI web-search and Perplexity Sonar accept country/city);
  - currency.
  
- Each collection repeats N times.
  - ==Daily run volume = P (intents, including category and need) × W (wording, language) × R (regions) × M (models) × N (repeats).==
