### Part 1: ESL

#### The Value of ESL to Retailers

The literature offers direct evidence for three distinct uses:
- (i) Saving the labour of relabelling
- (ii) Marking down fresh and near-expiry products 
- (iii) Supporting an existing pricing strategy 

#### First paper: The Effect of ESL Rollout on Banner Efficiency

Counterfactual: how a banner would have performed had it not rolled out ESL. 

**Setup.**
- **Dependent variables.** Sales per square metre; year-on-year sales growth (%).
- **Treatment.** The year in which ESL is rolled out to the majority of a banner's stores.

**Variables.**

*Outcome variables*

| Variable | Note |
|---|---|
| Sales per sqm | Banner efficiency. |
| Year-on-year sales growth (%) | Change in sales. |

*ESL rollout*

| Variable                           | Note                                    |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Majority-rollout year              | Treatment timing.                       |
| Scope (fresh only vs. whole store) | Gains concentrate in fresh.             |
| Label vendor                       | Vendors may offer different technology. |

*Banner characteristics*

| Variable                | Note                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Size                    | Capacity to invest.                                                  |
| Market share            | Market position.                                                     |
| Number of stores        | More stores, more relabelling saved.                                 |
| Average store size      | More labels per store.                                               |
| Format                  | Different assortment and pricing. Hypermarket/supermarket/discounter |
| Pricing strategy        | EDLP and Hi-Lo use ESL differently.                                  |
| Share of fresh products | More perishables to mark down.                                       |
| Prior-year sales growth | Banners already growing may adopt earlier.                           |

*Group characteristics*

| Variable                                                   | Note                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Parent group has already rolled out ESL in another country | Experience within the group. |

*External environment*

| Variable                                                       | Note                            |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Share of domestic competitors that have already rolled out ESL | Competitive pressure.           |
| Food inflation                                                 | More frequent price changes.    |
| Labour cost (minimum wage)                                     | Relabelling labour is costlier. |


### Part 2: LLM Brand Recommendations, Two Reference Papers

#### Malthouse, Lee & Yang (2026)
- The set of brands an LLM recommends is neither fixed nor directly observable; repeated requests can return different brands and rankings.
- Already-popular items can receive disproportionate exposure, while long-tail alternatives receive less.
- BRP@k: the share of repeated recommendation lists in which a brand appears among the first k.
- Adding the consumer's needs to the prompt can bring omitted brands back, but only partly (especially L.L.Bean). Probes written in the brands' own marketing language raise recall sharply.
- Setup: each request is a new, stateless API call with web search disabled. Our pilot forces web search on.
- To discuss: they justify their categories (high involvement, diversity, benchmark data). 

#### Aggarwal et al. (2024), GEO
- Rewriting web pages with nine "recipes" shows that adding statistics, quotations and source citations works best (a page's share of the answer rises from about 19% to about 27%); keyword stuffing does not help.
- For our study: for the brands an AI repeatedly recommends, what content features do their cited pages (brand sites, review sites, retailer pages) share?
