### The Value of ESL to Retailers

Electronic shelf labels (ESL) can appear, at first sight, to be a single technology with a single payoff. Yet the literature offers direct evidence for three distinct uses, and each one is likely to matter more for some retailers than for others:

- (i) **Saving the labour of relabelling**
- (ii) **Marking down fresh and near-expiry products** 
- (iii) **Supporting an existing pricing strategy** 

### Goal: The Effect of ESL Rollout on Banner Efficiency

Counterfactual: how a banner would have performed had it not rolled out ESL. This  guides the setup below.

**Setup.**
- **Dependent variables.** Sales per square metre; year-on-year sales growth (%).
- **Treatment.** The year in which ESL is rolled out to the majority of a banner's stores.
- **Sample.** (i) The top 15 banners by store-based sales in each country (following Bombaij & Dekimpe 2020); (ii) all banners searched so far.
- **Standard errors.** Clustered by parent group, or two-way clustered by banner and year (following Keller et al. 2016).

**Variables.**
- **Outcome variables.** Sales per square metre; year-on-year sales growth (%).
- **ESL rollout.** Majority-rollout year; scope (fresh only vs. whole store); label vendor.
- **Banner characteristics.** Size; market share; number of stores; average store size; format; pricing strategy; share of fresh products; prior-year sales growth.
- **Group characteristics.** Whether the parent group has already rolled out ESL in another country.
- **External environment.** Share of domestic competitors that have already rolled out ESL; food inflation; labour cost (minimum wage).
